# Reading the battle: what each formation should look like

For H-03 testers and for anyone checking the battle drawings. Each clip shows one marked formation over six frames, 30 battle seconds apart (about 2.5 minutes of fighting). Judge the formation across the loop, not from one frame, and judge only the marked one.

## Marks and symbols

- **Yellow pointer:** a downward yellow triangle above the marked formation. It marks the formation directly under its point. Nothing else on the picture is the mark. Place names (such as "the ford") are only labels.
- **Solid block:** a formation seen now (observed).
- **Dashed or hollow outline:** a formation as it was reported, so it may be out of date or wrong. Use this for the "seen now or reported" question.
- **Blue and red:** blue is the crown (the queen's side); red is the raiders.

## The seven behaviours

| Behaviour | What it looks like | What it means in the battle |
|---|---|---|
| In good order | One solid, unbroken block, banner upright, men in a line. | Men are together and obeying orders. |
| Yielding ground | Solid block, slightly shorter, banner tilted back, small chevrons (V shapes) under it. | In contact with the enemy, losing men, still holding a line but giving ground slowly. |
| Becoming disorganised | The block breaks into several uneven pieces, banner leaning. | Men are still there but control is slipping. Usually not running. |
| Officers rallying men | Men gathered in a tight cluster round an upright banner, with a dashed ring turning around them. | A formation that had broken is being pulled back together by nearby officers. |
| Withdrawing in order | Intact solid block with one straight arrow pointing back toward its own side. | An ordered pull-back on orders. Men stay together. |
| Uncontrolled retreat | Stretched, ragged outline with a jagged arrow streaming back toward its own side. | The line has broken and is falling back without control, but some shape remains. |
| Rout | No block and no banner. Scattered dots running away. | Men are fleeing and have lost all control. |

## Telling similar ones apart

- **Withdrawing or retreat:** withdrawing is intact with a neat arrow; retreat is ragged and loose.
- **Retreat or rout:** retreat still has a shape, however ragged; rout has no shape at all.
- **Yielding or disorganised:** yielding is one block getting shorter; disorganised is the block broken into pieces, usually standing still.
- **Yielding or withdrawing:** yielding is still facing the enemy in contact; withdrawing is moving away.
- **Rallying:** the dashed ring and the cluster round the banner are the cue. It is not a marker; a rallying formation can sit next to a marked one.

## Movement over the loop

- Good order, yielding, disorganised and rallying formations mostly stay where they are.
- Withdrawing formations move steadily back.
- Retreat and rout formations move back and break up as they go.
- A rally can happen in place: the men gather, the ring appears, the block reforms.

## Not in this test

- No sound. Every cue is visual.
- "Gone" (a formation that has left the field or is too small to fight) is not a behaviour in the test, so it does not appear in clips.

## Reporting a deviation

If a clip does not match this guide, note the clip id (for example "rout-2"), what you saw, and what you expected from this page. Those reports are the finding. The guide is written from the battle rules and the drawings in the code, so a mismatch may mean the drawing or the rule is wrong.
