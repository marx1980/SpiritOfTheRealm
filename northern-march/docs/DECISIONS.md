## D-040 — Wider economy, stage 2: levy and manpower (design, before building)
Stage 1 is committed (D-039; suite 205/205; KI-01 hash re-recorded). Stage 2 gives iron and horses a use and puts a price on soldiers.
- Manpower: each community has `hands` (men of fighting age available). Men away on service are not at the harvest.
- Muster order (levy with a `men` constraint, target a community, deliverTo a fort): the word reaches the community by rider; men = min(musterMen, hands above a reserve, iron at the fort / ironPerMan) × the community's recruiting cooperation (its stance, as for garrison recovery). Up to mountedPermille of them take horses from the fort's store. No iron → abandoned "noIron"; hands at the reserve → "noHands".
- Men and horses arrive at the fort on foot and add to its garrison above establishment (a mounted man counts as two). Wages are paid from the treasury every day (wageMilli per man) for serviceDays; then the surplus above establishment is discharged and the hands come back. Hands regenerate slowly toward their base.
- Costs of mustering besides coin: a levy grievance and trust loss in the community (scaled by the share of its hands taken), and a smaller harvest there while its men are away (harvestPermille of the share away). Both read from the existing communities and grain machinery; the coin levy is unchanged.
- Additive: with nothing mustered, hands are full and every number matches stage 1.
Tests first: garrison, hands, iron and horses move exactly (hands + away = base; iron and horses conserved in the ledger); noIron and noHands; wages debit daily and end at serviceDays, with discharge and return; harvest falls while men are away and recovers; a levy grievance follows the muster and a hostile community gives fewer men; nothing mustered, nothing changes.
Not in stage 2: advisers do not advise on mustering; no page affordance; battle strength counts garrison only (mounted weight is by the two-for-one rule above, not a separate cavalry model).

## D-041 — Wider economy, stage 3: House control of resources and markets (design, before building)
Stage 2 is committed (D-040). Stage 3 adds who owns the goods and where they can be sold.
- House control: every place that holds goods names its controlling House (crown by default). Sources: crownwood crown; pinegate veyr (timber); highcairn varos (iron); thornfield caron (horses). Fort depots are the crown's.
- The crown cannot requisition from a place another House controls (abandoned "houseHolds"); it can only buy there, at the realm price plus houseMarkupPermille (150). ## D-060 — Phase 8 design: the minimal battle slice (9 Nov, before code)
Scope from the plan (Phase 8) and BATTLEFIELD §2A-§24. Decisions:
- **A battle is a deterministic sub-simulation** (`src/battlefield/`), pure: the same setup and the same battle orders give the same battle. It steps every 10 game seconds (the plan's wake budget: one tactical replan per commander per 10 s) on the shared clock (1 tick = 1 s). The campaign stores only the setup (`BattleBegan`), the ruler's battle orders (player input, saved and replayed like any other) and the summary (`BattleEnded`); the full record (reality frames, beliefs, messengers, traces) is recomputed from them when needed, so the log stays small and every battle can be replayed on the page.
- **Entry** (§2B): when a sortie makes contact with raiders that are really there. The sortie's single roll is kept only when content has no `battle.field` (old variants). Forces carry over: the fort's garrison and every captain's company present, split into formations by content (`battle.formations`), and the raiders' men from their intensity (with the ambush band as before), split into warbands. Commanders: the characters present who command men (captain, castellan, sergeant) - up to three - and Anna with her guard if she is at that fort. Night is excluded (§24.4): a contact after dusk waits for dawn; nightfall ends the battle.
- **Reality vs beliefs**: every observer (headquarters and each commander) keeps sightings of formations; headquarters' map marks each as observed (seen now), reported (from a runner's report), inferred (headquarters' assumption that a delivered order is being carried out) and stale (older than 5 minutes), with age. Line of sight is cut by the wood, shortened by rain and by the dust of melee.
- **Orders travel** (§9): by runner to where headquarters believes the commander is; if he is not there the runner must find him, or fails; runners near the enemy can be killed (drawn only while in danger). Signals (§9A, §24.3) carry only pre-agreed meanings, need sight of headquarters and can be missed in smoke or rain. A formation keeps its instruction until a new one is received and accepted.
- **Receipt, interpretation, obedience** (§10, §11, ARCHITECTURE §20.8): comprehension = clarity of the order x the commander's military skill; below a threshold the order is read literally (limits and conditions dropped, traced). Obedience is scored, not rolled: obey, adapt or keep his current task, each scored from his own beliefs (local odds he believes, his losses) and traits (risk, commitment, loss response), with duty from his loyalty to the issuer; the decision uses the same margins and `decideObedience` as campaign orders. Each decision is a trace with reasons.
- **One formation behaviour model** (§13): hidden cohesion and fatigue drive seven visible behaviours - in good order, yielding ground, becoming disorganised, officers rallying men, withdrawing in order, uncontrolled retreat, rout. Troops react to what neighbours appear to do: an unannounced withdrawal looks like a retreat to a neighbour (§14).
- **Anna** (§2A): present only if she is at the fort; her guard is a formation. Danger is signalled (arrows near, aides urging withdrawal, the guard forming up, the line before her wavering) before any lethal draw, which happens only while a real threat is in contact with her guard. Death or capture ends the campaign.

## D-061 — Phase 8 built: what changed from the design while building (9 Nov)
- **Spacing and frontage.** Formations take up ground: friends keep 150 m apart, enemies meet at 36 m (`tactics.spacing`). Without it, attackers piled onto one target and the picture could not be read (seen on the first clip sheet).
- **Raider bands have war leaders.** Each band counts as led (the commander bonus to cohesion); only the crown's formations depend on a named commander being within voice range. Without it every band but the chief's lost nerve steadily and the crown won against any numbers.
- **Nerve and the odds.** Being outnumbered where they stand costs cohesion in proportion to the odds (up to five times the base), so 40 men on a ridge do not hold off 400 for hours.
- **Measured on the sample battle at Northwatch** (Bren, Edda, Caron: about 320 men; 20 seeds each, Anna absent): the crown wins every battle against 120-260 raiders, 19 of 20 against 360, none against 520. Not calibrated for balance; the threshold is sharp.
- **The queen.** Her guard falls back to the gate when her aides first urge withdrawal (400 m), unless she chose where to stand after that warning; within 180 m of the gate she is under the walls and the field cannot harm her (the campaign's raided-fort rule applies instead). Measured: default, 0 of 240 sample battles harmed her; insisting on standing forward against 520 raiders, 31 of 40 ended in her death or capture, every time after a warning.
- **Scripted rulers** leave battles to the plan: a battle pause with a policy other than `external` asks the policy nothing (the decision is still counted, so saves replay).
- **Reports** from commanders who dislike bad news (bad-news reporting below 450) soften their own men's state and report half as often; debriefs scale own losses by (400 + frankness)/1000 and the enemy's by (700 + 0.6 x risk)/1000.
- **The page.** Battle pauses show the field with headquarters' marks (solid: seen now; hollow and dashed: reported, with age; dotted: inferred; faded: stale), the commanders' orders as headquarters believes them with status and last word, reports and warnings, and a menu order form (runner, signal, or voice before the fight). `#battle` plays the opening silently to the first battle (not saved). H-03 clip page published separately (`docs/H03_PROTOCOL.md`).
- **Campaign effect** (10 seeds x 160 days, after tuning): aggressive 48 battles, raiders won 27; delegating 56 battles, raiders won 33, one drawn. Most sorties are castellans going out with the garrison alone against a larger band, chosen by the agents' own (pre-battle) estimate of a sortie's chances. Earlier sealed gates are expected to move; they are re-run on fresh blocks after the suite (D-062).

## D-063 — Pursuit, state words and the H-03 rewrite (12 Nov)
- **7A on block 310000 (D-062), recorded properly.** G-03: delegating PASS (6.7%, CI 5.2-8.2), aggressive PASS (22.2%, CI 19.7-24.9), passive FAIL (0.9%, CI 0.4-1.6; override 1 stands). Outcome shares pooled: cooperative support 16.0%, material success with political damage 23.5%, misinformation failure 7.1% pass; political_caution_military_danger 3.7% under the 5% floor (override 2 stands). 0 invariant violations. So 7A held after battles came in, as in D-058; the commit that filed the result called the two overridden items "FAIL", which overstated it. The rest of the D-062 round (blocks 320000 and 330000, tune-seed checks) was not run: the code changed first (below), so the round restarts on fresh blocks (D-064) and 310000 stands only for the code before pursuit.
- **Pursuit, an eighth behaviour** (the user's call: "add it now"). Men who see an enemy formation break (retreat or rout, within 300 m) run after it out of formation when their urge beats what holds them back, scored without dice (`wouldPursue`): urge = (1000 - discipline) + half the commander's risk + 200 if mounted; restraint = half his skill if he is within voice range + 300 if their task is to keep ground (hold, protect, give ground) + their fatigue; starting needs a margin of 100, keeping on needs none. Once running they keep after the same men until those reform, get 600 m away, cross a line they were told not to cross, or are gone. The guard never pursues. Costs: pursuers take harm as if disorganised (so horse hits them harder), tire twice as fast while moving, lose 6 cohesion a step when shot at or losing a fight, and leave their place. A `limit` line now also holds against the press of other formations (`keepLimit`): before, friends and enemies could shove a formation across a stream it was told not to cross. Measured before the fatigue term: crown units spent 29% of their time pursuing, foot following foot routers to the field's edge; with it, 10% (raiders 5%).
- **Measured on the sample battle at Northwatch** (40 seeds x 180/360/520 raiders x Anna absent or in the centre, no player orders; 240 battles): crown won 148 / raiders 78 / none 14 before pursuit; 146 / 83 / 11 after. Losses per battle: crown 69 to 63, raiders 69 to 62; mean length 116 to 97 minutes. Anna safe in all 120 with her. Not calibrated for balance.
- **Test change:** "no agent flips between plans" now treats going back after a sortie that was fought as finished, not dithering (its own comment said so; a battle can settle a sortie within hours, which the old roll never showed).
- **State words.** Every state but good order is written on the unit (Yielding, Disorganised, Rallying, Withdrawing, Retreat, Rout, Pursuing), from headquarters' belief, drawn as observed or reported like the unit and carrying a report's age. Reasoning: the incomplete information in this game is about what headquarters knows (seen, reported, how old, how honest), not about decoding symbols; a routing unit in plain view is not hard to read. Words are drawn above names, names above units. Report ages read "<1 min" instead of "now".
- **Colours and flash.** Raiders are drawn dark red (#a0241c) in both themes so a light pink flash (#ffb6d9) belongs to neither side. Units seen breaking (observed retreat or rout) can flash once a second to light pink or to a pale tint of their own colour; viewers who reduce motion get half the rate rather than none. The game page shows no flash until H-03 picks a style.
- **Framing.** The battle view frames the units seen now plus headquarters (at least 1,400 m wide in the game, 900 m in the test), square on narrow screens, instead of the whole 2,400 m field. Measured first: at full field, names and words were about 8 px on desktop and unreadable on a phone. Old reports may fall outside the frame.
- **H-03 rewritten** (`docs/H03_PROTOCOL.md`, version 2): with words on units, naming a behaviour from its shape no longer tests anything. The test is now about play: tap our unit in trouble (timed), say whose an outlined breaking unit is, rate crowding per style, tell seen-now from a report; three styles (no flash, pink, pale) rotated between testers. Bars proposed: trouble found ≥ 90% and side ≥ 95% in the chosen style, seen-or-report ≥ 90%, crowding ≤ 3 of 5. Version 1 was replaced before any tester finished it. The test page outlines the unit a question is about (a dashed box holding only that unit) instead of the triangle pointer, which left it unclear which of two close units was meant.
