# Northern March Interaction Prototype

## Battlefield concept
Real-time command-and-control from Anna's physical command position, not an omniscient RTS camera.

## Information
Observed, reported, inferred and unknown are distinct. Terrain, smoke, weather, distance and concealment determine visibility. Friendly formations can also become uncertain.

## Conversation
Player can ask what a general would do, why, alternatives and risks. Better generals produce better diagnosis and contingencies, challenge bad ideas and recognise uncertainty. Natural affirmation issues the mutually understood structured order.

## Orders
Orders travel physically. Formations continue previous instructions until new orders arrive and propagate. Receipt, understanding, willingness to obey and execution skill are separate.

## Human friction
Pride, ambition, stubbornness, rivalry, loyalty, local interests and political motives can produce adaptation or deliberate disobedience. Disobedience can save or ruin the battle.

## Formations
Training, discipline, fatigue, casualties, cohesion, nearby behaviour and officer quality determine performance. Morale is shown through behaviour, not bars. Ordered retreat can be mistaken for rout.

## Prototype stake
Anna's death ends the campaign. Moving forward improves visibility and command responsiveness but increases campaign-ending danger.
