# H-03 Battle screen playability — protocol (Phase 8, version 3, D-066)

**Question.** At real size, on a phone and on a desktop, can a player read the battle screen: find what matters, find a unit by what it is, and tell what is seen now from a report, without the screen being too crowded?

**Page.** The Battle Clips artifact. Each tester gets every item once, part by part, in an order of their own. Answers are stored per tester with the stamp of the clip set they were given on; answers on another set are kept aside, not counted.

**Parts.**
1. In trouble (10 clips, six pictures each, 30 battle seconds apart): the busiest moments in which one or two of our units are seen breaking. Tap one. Timed.
2. Find (12 pictures): tap our cavalry (4), Caron's company (4), the unit of ours under arrows (4). Any unit that fits counts.
3. Seen or reported (12 pictures, 6 each): the outlined unit is fully in view and clear of others.
After each part: rate crowding 1 (clear) to 5 (crowded), and note what overlapped or hid something.

**Drawing tested.** Our units only carry state words; words and report ages make room for each other and for units; units seen breaking flash pink; volleys drawn; the view framed on the busiest cluster.

**Bars (confirmed by Marko for version 2, carried over):** in trouble at least 90%; find at least 90% overall and at least 75% for each of the three; seen or reported at least 90%; mean crowding no worse than 3 in every part. Below a bar: report the numbers and the notes, change the drawing (not the bar), and run again on new testers. Not passed on fewer than 5 finished testers, at least 2 on a phone.

**Version 2 (13 Nov, one tester, Marko, not scorable):** clips were re-cut during his run, so his spot answers were scored against other clips. Usable findings: side told 12/12 in every style (dropped); crowding rated 1 in all three flash styles (pink kept); seen-or-report 10/12, mistakes where the unit was partly off screen or covered; spotting a unit in trouble was obvious; labels were in the way.
